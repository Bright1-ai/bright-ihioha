How to map CAC across search, social, and outbound

CAC is not a channel leaderboard. It is a shared language for deciding which acquisition work deserves more attention.

By Ihioha Bright2026-08-136 min read
Share this note
LinkedInPost on XEmail link
Short answer

Start by defining one qualified-opportunity language across every channel. Then measure the cost of creating that opportunity, the quality of the pipeline it creates, and the revenue signal that follows.

The question

How should an early-stage B2B SaaS team compare CAC across search, paid social, and outbound?

CACAttributionB2B SaaS

Begin with the opportunity, not the click

Channel reporting becomes noisy when each team uses a different definition of progress. A search campaign may count a form fill, a social campaign may count a landing-page view, and an outbound system may count a reply. Those events can all be useful, but they are not interchangeable.

Choose a shared point in the buying journey: for example, a qualified opportunity that matches the product’s customer profile and has a credible next step. The exact definition belongs to the business. The important part is that it is written down and used consistently.

  • Define the account or user profile that qualifies.
  • Define the evidence of active need.
  • Define the next step that makes the opportunity real.

Use one measurement spine across channels

Once the opportunity is defined, map every channel into the same spine: spend, source, qualified opportunities, sales progression, and revenue. This does not make attribution perfect. It makes the assumptions visible enough to improve.

Search often captures existing intent. Social can create or shape interest. Outbound can introduce a problem to a specific account. Their roles differ, so the useful comparison is not only cost per lead; it is the quality and speed of the opportunity each channel helps create.

What this framework does not claim

A simple CAC map cannot prove that one channel caused every downstream outcome. It is a decision tool, not a promise of a universal benchmark. Use it to surface better questions: which sources create the right conversations, where does the funnel lose context, and which evidence should change the next experiment?
